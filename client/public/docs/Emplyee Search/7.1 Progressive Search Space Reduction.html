7.1 Progressive Search Space Reduction

One of the most significant engineering decisions made during development was recognizing that not every search algorithm should examine every record.

Although each algorithm was capable of identifying particular classes of matches, their computational costs varied considerably. Applying the most computationally expensive methods indiscriminately across the complete dataset produced little practical benefit while significantly increasing execution time.

Instead, the search process gradually evolved into a sequence of progressively refined filters.

Computationally inexpensive operations were executed first to eliminate obviously unrelated records. More sophisticated similarity algorithms were then applied only to the smaller candidate set that remained.

This approach substantially reduced the amount of work performed by the expensive algorithms without sacrificing search quality.

The principle resembles multi-stage filtering systems commonly found throughout engineering disciplines. Rather than immediately applying the most precise analytical method, successive filtering stages progressively reduce the search space until only the most relevant candidates remain.

Although the implementation evolved through practical experimentation rather than formal optimization, this progressive filtering strategy ultimately became one of the most influential design decisions within the system.

Engineering Observation

Search performance depends not only upon the efficiency of individual algorithms but also upon the number of records they are required to evaluate.

Reducing the candidate set before invoking computationally expensive algorithms frequently produces greater performance improvements than attempting to optimize the expensive algorithm itself.

Engineering Principle

Reducing the search space is often more effective than accelerating the search algorithm.
